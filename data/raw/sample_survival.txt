WATER PURIFICATION AFTER DISASTERS

Contaminated water is a major health risk after natural disasters. Proper purification is essential for survival.

IMMEDIATE STEPS:
1. Filter water through clean cloth to remove debris
2. Boil water for at least 1 minute (3 minutes at high altitude)
3. Add 8 drops of household bleach per gallon of water
4. Let stand for 30 minutes before drinking
5. Store purified water in clean, covered containers

DO NOT:
- Drink untreated water from unknown sources
- Use cloudy or discolored water
- Assume water is safe after boiling alone
- Store water in containers that held chemicals
- Use water purification tablets past expiration date

WHEN TO SEEK HELP:
- Signs of waterborne illness (diarrhea, vomiting)
- No access to fuel for boiling
- Chemical contamination suspected
- Severe dehydration symptoms

EMERGENCY SHELTER CONSTRUCTION

Building emergency shelter can mean the difference between life and death in extreme conditions.

IMMEDIATE STEPS:
1. Choose high ground away from flood zones
2. Use natural features (caves, overhangs) when possible
3. Build frame using available materials (branches, debris)
4. Create waterproof roof with plastic, leaves, or bark
5. Insulate floor with dry leaves or clothing
6. Ensure adequate ventilation
7. Build shelter large enough to lie down comfortably

DO NOT:
- Build shelter in low-lying areas
- Use unstable or heavy materials overhead
- Block all ventilation
- Build too close to water sources
- Ignore weather conditions

WHEN TO SEEK HELP:
- Severe weather conditions
- No suitable materials available
- Medical emergency requiring shelter
- Group of people needing coordinated shelter

SAFE FIRE STARTING TECHNIQUES

Fire provides warmth, light, and cooking capability in survival situations.

IMMEDIATE STEPS:
1. Choose safe location away from flammable materials
2. Clear area of debris in 3-foot radius
3. Gather tinder (dry grass, paper, bark)
4. Build small teepee structure with kindling
5. Use fire starter, matches, or friction method
6. Add larger fuel gradually
7. Keep fire contained and supervised

DO NOT:
- Start fire in dry, windy conditions
- Use accelerants or gasoline
- Leave fire unattended
- Build fire too large for needs
- Start fire near shelter or food storage

WHEN TO SEEK HELP:
- Fire spreads beyond control
- No suitable fire-starting materials
- Severe weather makes fire dangerous
- Medical emergency requiring immediate warmth

EMERGENCY FOOD SAFETY

Knowing what food is safe to eat during disasters prevents illness and maintains strength.

IMMEDIATE STEPS:
1. Eat canned goods within 2 years of expiration
2. Consume refrigerated food within 4 hours of power loss
3. Cook all meat and eggs thoroughly
4. Avoid food with unusual odor or appearance
5. Prioritize high-calorie, non-perishable items
6. Stay hydrated while eating

DO NOT:
- Eat food from damaged cans
- Consume food that smells or looks spoiled
- Eat raw meat or eggs
- Ignore expiration dates on critical items
- Eat large amounts of unfamiliar foods

WHEN TO SEEK HELP:
- Signs of food poisoning
- No access to safe food sources
- Severe malnutrition symptoms
- Allergic reactions to emergency rations

SANITATION WITHOUT RUNNING WATER

Maintaining hygiene prevents disease in emergency situations.

IMMEDIATE STEPS:
1. Use hand sanitizer or soap with minimal water
2. Bury human waste 6-8 inches deep, 200 feet from water
3. Wash hands after bathroom use
4. Keep living area clean and organized
5. Dispose of garbage properly
6. Change clothes regularly if possible

DO NOT:
- Contaminate water sources with waste
- Ignore hand hygiene
- Allow garbage to accumulate
- Use contaminated water for cleaning
- Share personal hygiene items

WHEN TO SEEK HELP:
- Signs of infection or illness
- No access to basic hygiene supplies
- Large group requiring sanitation coordination
- Medical conditions requiring special care
