EMERGENCY CHECK-IN MESSAGES

Regular check-ins during disasters help family and emergency responders track your safety and location.

IMMEDIATE STEPS:
1. Send brief status update every 4-6 hours
2. Include current location and safety status
3. Mention any immediate needs or injuries
4. Use simple, clear language
5. Keep messages under 160 characters when possible
6. Establish check-in schedule with family

DO NOT:
- Send unnecessary long messages
- Share sensitive personal information
- Ignore family check-in requests
- Use complex language or abbreviations
- Delay check-ins during critical situations

WHEN TO SEEK HELP:
- Unable to send messages
- Family doesn't respond to check-ins
- Emergency situation requires immediate assistance
- Communication devices malfunction

EMERGENCY CONTACT TEMPLATES

Having pre-written emergency messages saves time and ensures clear communication during crises.

IMMEDIATE STEPS:
1. Create contact list with emergency numbers
2. Write template messages for common situations
3. Include your name, location, and emergency type
4. Specify what help you need
5. Mention any medical conditions or special needs
6. Keep copies in multiple locations

DO NOT:
- Share emergency contacts publicly
- Use vague or unclear language
- Forget to update contact information
- Rely on single communication method
- Delay sending emergency messages

WHEN TO SEEK HELP:
- No response from emergency contacts
- Situation becomes life-threatening
- Communication systems fail
- Medical emergency requiring immediate response

SITUATION STATUS REPORTS

Clear status reports help emergency responders understand your situation and provide appropriate assistance.

IMMEDIATE STEPS:
1. Assess your current situation calmly
2. Note any injuries or medical needs
3. Identify immediate threats or dangers
4. List available resources and supplies
5. Estimate how long you can remain safe
6. Communicate clearly and concisely

DO NOT:
- Panic or use emotional language
- Exaggerate or minimize your situation
- Forget to mention critical details
- Ignore changing circumstances
- Refuse help when offered

WHEN TO SEEK HELP:
- Situation becomes unsafe
- Medical condition worsens
- Supplies running low
- Weather or conditions deteriorating

ASSISTANCE REQUEST MESSAGES

Knowing how to request help effectively can save lives during emergencies.

IMMEDIATE STEPS:
1. Clearly state what help you need
2. Provide your exact location
3. Mention any time-sensitive factors
4. List available resources you have
5. Specify any special requirements
6. Keep message brief but complete

DO NOT:
- Send multiple requests for same help
- Use vague or unclear language
- Forget to mention critical details
- Ignore safety instructions from responders
- Panic or use emotional language

WHEN TO SEEK HELP:
- No response to assistance request
- Situation becomes immediately dangerous
- Medical emergency requiring immediate care
- Communication systems fail completely

EVACUATION NOTIFICATIONS

Clear evacuation messages help family and emergency services coordinate during disasters.

IMMEDIATE STEPS:
1. Notify family of evacuation decision
2. Specify destination and route
3. Mention expected arrival time
4. List any special needs or requirements
5. Provide contact information for destination
6. Update status upon arrival

DO NOT:
- Evacuate without notifying family
- Use unsafe evacuation routes
- Ignore official evacuation orders
- Forget essential supplies or documents
- Delay evacuation in dangerous conditions

WHEN TO SEEK HELP:
- Unable to reach evacuation destination
- Route becomes blocked or dangerous
- Vehicle breaks down during evacuation
- Medical emergency during travel
