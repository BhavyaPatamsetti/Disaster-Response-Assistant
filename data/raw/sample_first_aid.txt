FIRST AID FOR HEAVY BLEEDING

Heavy bleeding is a life-threatening emergency that requires immediate attention. Blood loss can lead to shock and death if not treated promptly.

IMMEDIATE STEPS:
1. Apply direct pressure to the wound using a clean cloth or sterile gauze
2. Elevate the injured area above the heart if possible
3. Maintain pressure for at least 10-15 minutes
4. If bleeding continues, apply additional layers of dressing
5. Call emergency services immediately

DO NOT:
- Remove soaked dressings (add more on top)
- Apply tourniquet unless absolutely necessary
- Clean the wound with harsh chemicals
- Delay seeking professional medical help

WHEN TO SEEK HELP:
- Bleeding doesn't stop after 15 minutes of direct pressure
- Blood is bright red and spurting (arterial bleeding)
- Person shows signs of shock (pale, cold, rapid breathing)
- Wound is deep or gaping
- Object is embedded in the wound

CPR FOR ADULTS

Cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) is a lifesaving technique used when someone's heart stops beating.

IMMEDIATE STEPS:
1. Check responsiveness - tap and shout "Are you okay?"
2. Call emergency services immediately
3. Check for breathing (look, listen, feel)
4. If not breathing, begin chest compressions
5. Place hands on center of chest, interlock fingers
6. Compress chest 2 inches deep at rate of 100-120 per minute
7. Give 2 rescue breaths after every 30 compressions

DO NOT:
- Stop compressions unless person shows signs of life
- Compress too shallow or too deep
- Give breaths if you're not trained
- Delay calling emergency services

WHEN TO SEEK HELP:
- Always call emergency services before starting CPR
- Continue until help arrives or person shows signs of life
- If you're tired, ask bystanders to take turns

TREATING BURNS

Burns are classified by severity and require different treatment approaches.

IMMEDIATE STEPS:
1. Remove person from source of burn
2. Cool the burn with cool (not cold) running water for 10-20 minutes
3. Remove jewelry and tight clothing from burned area
4. Cover with sterile, non-stick bandage
5. Do not break blisters
6. Keep person warm and comfortable

DO NOT:
- Apply ice directly to burn
- Use butter, oil, or home remedies
- Pop blisters
- Apply adhesive bandages to large burns
- Give pain medication without medical advice

WHEN TO SEEK HELP:
- Burns larger than 3 inches in diameter
- Burns on face, hands, feet, or genitals
- Third-degree burns (white or charred skin)
- Burns caused by chemicals or electricity
- Signs of infection (redness, swelling, pus)

FRACTURE FIRST AID

Broken bones require immediate attention to prevent further injury and complications.

IMMEDIATE STEPS:
1. Stop any bleeding first
2. Immobilize the injured area
3. Apply ice pack wrapped in cloth to reduce swelling
4. Elevate the injured limb if possible
5. Keep person still and comfortable
6. Call emergency services for severe fractures

DO NOT:
- Try to straighten or realign the bone
- Move person with suspected neck or back injury
- Apply heat to the injury
- Give food or drink if surgery might be needed
- Delay seeking medical attention

WHEN TO SEEK HELP:
- Bone is visible through skin
- Person cannot move the injured area
- Severe pain or swelling
- Deformity or unusual angle of limb
- Numbness or tingling below injury
